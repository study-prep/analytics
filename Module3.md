### #173

* **Answer Hint:** Decision Node (Square - points of active choice) and Chance Node (Circle - points of probabilistic external events).

### #175
* **Question:** Explain how a decision tree can help a manager choose between different business alternatives under uncertainty.
* **Answer Hint:** It structures complex possibilities visually and mathematically weights payoffs by their likelihood, establishing clear risk-adjusted values.

### #176
* **Question:** Describe the basic steps involved in using a decision tree.
* **Answer Hint:** (1) Define decision alternatives, (2) Map chance events and probabilities, (3) Assign payoffs at terminal nodes, and (4) Calculate EMV using backward rollback.

### #177
* **Question:** Define the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) and state its main purpose.
* **Answer Hint:** AHP (developed by Thomas Saaty) is a multi-criteria decision tool decomposing complex problems into a hierarchy and using pairwise comparisons to rank choices.

### #178
* **Question:** List any two important components considered in the Analytical Hierarchy Process.
* **Answer Hint:** Evaluation Criteria (standards for judgment) and Decision Alternatives (the competing choices evaluated).

### #179
* **Question:** Explain how AHP can help a manager select the most suitable option using multiple criteria (e.g., cost, quality, risk).
* **Answer Hint:** It establishes priority weights for criteria via pairwise comparison matrices (1–9 scale) and scores alternatives to provide an objective composite ranking.

### #180
* **Question:** Describe the purpose of sensitivity analysis when evaluating business decisions.
* **Answer Hint:** It tests the robustness of a decision by observing how changes in key variables (e.g., price, demand, cost) affect the final payoff or project NPV.

### #181
* **Question:** Analyze how a decision tree can help a company evaluate alternatives with uncertain profit or loss outcomes.
* **Answer Hint:** By calculating EMV = $\sum (P_i \times \text{Payoff}_i)$, it balances high-return high-risk paths against safe lower-return paths objectively.

### #182
* **Question:** Analyze how sensitivity analysis can help a manager identify which factors are most likely to change the result of a decision.
* **Answer Hint:** It isolates sensitive "swing" variables, demonstrating whether a slight decrease in sales volume turns positive profits into critical losses.

### #183
* **Question:** Evaluate the usefulness of AHP for a business manager balancing cost, quality, risk, and customer satisfaction.
* **Answer Hint:** AHP synthesizes tangible financial metrics with intangible qualitative values, ensuring mathematically consistent and justifiable trade-offs.

### #184
* **Question:** Evaluate how scenario planning can support a manager making a strategic decision under high uncertainty.
* **Answer Hint:** Instead of betting on a single flawed forecast, it builds multiple plausible operational futures (best-case, base-case, worst-case), building organizational resilience.

## Long Answer Questions (5 Marks)

### #229
* **Question:** Define a Decision Tree and identify its three standard components (Decision Nodes, Chance/Probability Nodes, and End/Payoff Nodes).
* **Answer Framework:**
  * **Definition:** A graphical quantitative decision-support model mapping chronological choices, probabilities, and financial outcomes.
  * **Decision Nodes (Squares):** Points of deliberate managerial choice.
  * **Chance Nodes (Circles):** Points of environmental uncertainty governed by mutually exclusive probabilities ($\sum P = 1.0$).
  * **Terminal Nodes (Triangles/Lines):** Endpoints displaying net financial payoffs.

### #230
* **Question:** State the basic definition of Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) and list the three primary levels in a standard AHP hierarchy.
* **Answer Framework:**
  * **Definition:** Saaty's multi-criteria decision framework using pairwise comparison matrices.
  * **Level 1 (Top):** Overall Goal/Objective (e.g., Selecting optimal vendor).
  * **Level 2 (Middle):** Criteria and Sub-criteria (Cost, Reliability, Lead time).
  * **Level 3 (Bottom):** Competing Alternatives (Vendor A, Vendor B, Vendor C).

### #231
* **Question:** Explain the fundamental purpose of Sensitivity Analysis in decision-making. Describe how testing changes in key assumptions helps a manager understand risk.
* **Answer Framework:**
  * Explain systematic testing of single variables while holding others constant.
  * Discuss finding project breakeven thresholds, identifying sensitive risk drivers, and focusing contingency resources.

### #232
* **Question:** Explain the concept and purpose of Scenario Planning in business strategy. Illustrate how developing best-case, base-case, and worst-case scenarios prepares an organization.
* **Answer Framework:**
  * Define plausible narrative futures vs deterministic forecasting.
  * **Best-Case:** Prepares scaling capacity and capital availability.
  * **Base-Case:** Sets operational budget and targets.
  * **Worst-Case:** Establishes emergency cash preservation triggers and safety margins.

### #233
* **Question:** Define the term "Expected Monetary Value (EMV)" as used in decision tree analysis and state the formula used to calculate it.
* **Answer Framework:**
  * **Definition:** The probability-weighted average payoff of an alternative under risk.
  * **Formula:** $\text{EMV} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (P_i \times X_i)$, where $P_i$ is probability of outcome $i$ and $X_i$ is financial payoff of outcome $i$.

### #234
* **Question:** Analyze the following business decision by drawing and interpreting a decision tree: Launch new product (60% high demand = $80,000 profit; 40% low demand = -$20,000 loss) vs. Continue existing product (90% = $30,000; 10% = $10,000).
* **Answer & Calculations:**
  * **EMV (Launch New Product):** $(0.60 \times \$80,000) + (0.40 \times -\$20,000) = \$48,000 - \$8,000 = \$40,000$
  * **EMV (Continue Existing):** $(0.90 \times \$30,000) + (0.10 \times \$10,000) = \$27,000 + \$1,000 = \$28,000$
  * **Recommendation:** The company should **launch the new product**, as its EMV of **$40,000** exceeds the existing product's EMV of **$28,000**.

### #235
* **Question:** Analyze the following decision: Introduce new service (70% high demand = $90,000; 30% low demand = -$30,000) vs. Continue current service (80% = $35,000; 20% = $15,000).
* **Answer & Calculations:**
  * **EMV (Introduce New Service):** $(0.70 \times \$90,000) + (0.30 \times -\$30,000) = \$63,000 - \$9,000 = \$54,000$
  * **EMV (Continue Current):** $(0.80 \times \$35,000) + (0.20 \times \$15,000) = \$28,000 + \$3,000 = \$31,000$
  * **Recommendation:** Choose to **introduce the new service** ($54,000 > $31,000).

### #236
* **Question:** Analyze the following decision: Open new store (65% high sales = $100,000; 35% low sales = -$40,000) vs. Expand existing store (75% = $50,000; 25% = $20,000).
* **Answer & Calculations:**
  * **EMV (Open New Store):** $(0.65 \times \$100,000) + (0.35 \times -\$40,000) = \$65,000 - \$14,000 = \$51,000$
  * **EMV (Expand Existing):** $(0.75 \times \$50,000) + (0.25 \times \$20,000) = \$37,500 + \$5,000 = \$42,500$
  * **Recommendation:** Choose to **open the new store** ($51,000 > $42,500).
