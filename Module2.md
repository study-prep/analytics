## Short Answer Questions (3 Marks)

### #161
* **Question:** Explain the meaning of decision framing in a business situation and describe why clearly framing a decision is important before selecting a solution.
* **Answer Hint:** Decision framing defines the context, boundaries, and perspective of a problem. It establishes the available alternatives, required data, and decision-maker preferences.

### #162
* **Question:** Describe the role of alternatives and information when a manager is trying to choose between different business options.
* **Answer Hint:** Alternatives represent actionable choices; information provides evidence of potential outcomes and risks, transforming guesswork into informed evaluation.

### #163
* **Question:** Define a heuristic in decision-making and explain how a manager may use a simple mental shortcut when making a decision quickly.
* **Answer Hint:** A heuristic is a cognitive rule-of-thumb that speeds up decision-making under high time pressure by focusing on key indicators rather than exhaustive computation.

### #164
* **Question:** Define bias in the context of decision-making and explain how a manager's personal beliefs or assumptions may influence the final decision.
* **Answer Hint:** A bias is a systematic deviation from objective rationality, causing decision-makers to overweight preconceptions or recent events.

### #165
* **Question:** Explain the main idea of the intuitive decision-making model and describe a situation where an experienced manager may use intuition.
* **Answer Hint:** Relies on tacit pattern recognition and subconscious synthesis of past experiences; useful in emergency crises (e.g., product recall, PR disaster).

### #166
* **Question:** Describe the main features of the rational decision-making model and explain how a manager can use a systematic process to select alternatives.
* **Answer Hint:** Follows an objective, step-by-step sequence: problem identification, criteria weighting, alternative generation, scoring, and optimal selection.

### #167
* **Question:** List any two important elements that a manager should consider while framing a business decision.
* **Answer Hint:** (1) The range of viable alternatives, and (2) The quality and reliability of available information (along with decision-maker value preferences).

### #168
* **Question:** List any two common factors that may influence a manager's decision-making process when choosing between alternatives.
* **Answer Hint:** (1) Cognitive biases / past personal experience, and (2) Time constraints and information availability.

### #169
* **Question:** Define bounded rationality and explain why a manager may not always be able to make a completely rational decision.
* **Answer Hint:** Formulated by Herbert Simon; human decision-makers face cognitive limits, incomplete information, and time pressures, leading to "satisficing" rather than optimizing.

### #170
* **Question:** List the four components of SWOT analysis that a manager can use to understand an organization.
* **Answer Hint:** Strengths (internal), Weaknesses (internal), Opportunities (external), and Threats (external).

### #171
* **Question:** Analyze how the use of heuristics and the presence of biases can influence the quality of a business decision.
* **Answer Hint:** While heuristics save time, unmonitored biases (such as overconfidence or anchoring) lead to inaccurate forecasts and neglected risk factors.

### #172
* **Question:** Compare the rational and intuitive decision-making models in a business situation.
* **Answer Hint:** Rational models offer structured rigor for high-stakes capital investments, while intuitive models offer speed and pattern recognition in time-sensitive emergencies.

## Long Answer Questions (5 Marks)

### #219
* **Question:** Explain the concept of bounded rationality in managerial decision-making. Discuss why human cognitive limits, time constraints, and incomplete information force managers to seek a "satisficing" outcome rather than a perfect one.
* **Answer Framework:** Detail Herbert Simon's theory. Explain the 3 constraints (cognitive limits, information asymmetry, and time pressure). Define "satisficing" (selecting the first option meeting acceptable thresholds) vs "optimizing".

### #220
* **Question:** Explain the difference between intuitive and rational decision-making models. Describe a practical scenario where a manager relies on gut feeling and another where a structured, step-by-step analytical model is necessary.
* **Answer Framework:** Contrast System 1 (fast, unconscious, associative) vs System 2 (slow, deliberate, analytical). Contrast emergency crisis management (intuitive) with multi-million dollar M&A valuation (rational DCF modeling).

### #221
* **Question:** Analyze how cognitive heuristics and biases distort managerial judgment. Using the examples of Confirmation Bias and Availability Heuristic, examine how each bias can lead a marketing team to misinterpret consumer feedback.
* **Answer Framework:**
  * **Confirmation Bias:** Overweighting positive reviews that validate expectations while ignoring product defects.
  * **Availability Heuristic:** Overreacting to a single viral customer complaint on social media while ignoring statistically sound survey satisfaction data.

### #222
* **Question:** Explain how decision framing transforms business and consumer choices with reference to a simple buying or pricing situation.
* **Answer Framework:** Show how context shifts risk perception. Compare an annual software cost ("₹3,650/year" - triggers loss aversion) vs daily micro-frame ("₹10/day - less than a cup of tea" - anchors against trivial expenditures).

### #223
* **Question:** Analyze a business scenario where a company must choose between hiring more staff or automating its customer service. Break down this dilemma using a Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA) structure across tangible costs, intangible costs, and expected benefits.
* **Answer Framework:** Compare Option A (Hiring Staff) vs Option B (AI Automation) across salaries, overhead, emotional empathy, 24/7 uptime, bot loop frustration, and scalability. Formulate the Net Benefit decision rule.

### #224
* **Question:** Evaluate the suitability of using a SWOT analysis for a new quick-service restaurant entering a competitive urban area. Assess the strengths and limitations of relying on SWOT alone.
* **Answer Framework:** Acknowledge diagnostic utility (internal kitchen efficiency vs delivery growth). Detail limitations: lacks factor weighting, static snapshot, descriptive without mathematical breakeven modeling, and vulnerable to confirmation bias.

### #225
* **Question:** A logistics company is deciding whether to make an urgent operational route change based on an experienced fleet manager's intuition or wait 24 hours for a complete data report. Evaluate both models and justify the decision under high time pressure.
* **Answer Framework:** Assess fleet manager pattern recognition vs 24-hr analytical paralysis. Conclude that under urgent SLA deadlines, the intuitive approach with real-time GPS telemetry validation is essential.

### #226
* **Question:** Assess the effectiveness of Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA) when deciding on an employee wellness program where outcomes (morale, loyalty) are non-financial.
* **Answer Framework:** Explain shadow pricing and financial proxies (reduced turnover replacement costs, reduced absenteeism days, lower health insurance premiums) to adapt CBA for qualitative programs.

### #227
* **Question:** Evaluate how the framing of a financial loss versus an equivalent financial gain influences executive risk-taking behavior. Justify using behavioral economics principles.
* **Answer Framework:** Apply Kahneman & Tversky's Prospect Theory. Explain the asymmetric S-shaped value curve: risk-averse in the domain of gains (locking in certain profit) and risk-seeking in the domain of losses (gambling to avoid guaranteed loss).

### #228
* **Question:** A retail firm faces declining store sales. One director recommends an intuitive rebranding campaign, while another demands a fully rational, data-heavy research study. Evaluate both and recommend a balanced path.
* **Answer Framework:** Contrast creative velocity vs diagnostic precision. Recommend an agile dual-track: 5-day POS transactional data audit to pinpoint churn causes, followed by quick A/B testing of rebranding concepts in select pilot stores.

### #250
* **Question:** Explain how biases can affect decision-making in a business and describe two common types of bias with suitable examples.
* **Answer Framework:** Explain systematic deviations from rationality. Describe Confirmation Bias and Anchoring Bias with business examples.

### #251
* **Question:** Define bias and identify two common types of bias in a hiring scenario where a manager favors an alumnus from their college and assumes younger candidates are tech-savvy.
* **Answer Framework:** Define bias. Identify (1) Similarity / In-group Bias (favoring alumni), and (2) Age-based Stereotyping (assuming technological proficiency without testing).

### #252
* **Question:** Explain the role of bias in a promotion scenario where a manager favors Employee B due to shared personal interests and overlooks Employee A for being quiet in meetings.
* **Answer Framework:** Identify Similarity Bias (shared personal hobbies) and Halo/Horns Effect or Extraversion Stereotype (confusing quiet demeanor with lack of leadership capability).
